## Purpose

The federal government is legally required to award a percentage of contracts to
Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Businesses (SDVOSB) and Veteran-Owned Small
Businesses (VOSB). The government regularly auctions surplus equipment, vehicles,
and property — veterans get priority access.

Most veterans who qualify have no idea any of this exists.

This division cuts through the red tape and gets veterans to the door.

---

## ⚑ Things That Will Surprise You — Read These First

These are the most underutilized, least-known programs in this entire division.
Even veterans who are deep in the space often miss them.

### The Rule of Two (VA-Specific — Legally Mandated)
Under 38 U.S.C. §8127–8128, the VA must award a contract to an SDVOSB if
**two or more** qualified SDVOSB businesses can perform the work at a fair price.
The VA cannot open it to full competition. They are legally required to award
to an SDVOSB first.

The VA is the second-largest federal agency by budget. They procure everything:
medical supplies, IT systems, construction, janitorial, food, transportation,
consulting, security. A certified SDVOSB in almost any industry has VA contract
opportunities available — most just don't know to look.

**This is not a preference. It is a legal mandate. Use it.**

### HUBZone — Stackable on Top of SDVOSB
If your business is located in a Historically Underutilized Business Zone
(rural areas, high-unemployment areas, Native American lands), HUBZone
certification gives additional contracting preferences. Many Western Slope
Colorado areas likely qualify.

Stack SDVOSB + HUBZone and you are in an extremely narrow competitive field.
Almost nobody does this. Check at: map.sba.gov/hubzone/maps

### SBA Mentor-Protégé Program — Solves the Past Performance Problem
The biggest barrier to federal contracting is past performance — you need
contracts to prove you can do the work, but you need past performance to
win contracts. This program breaks that loop.

An established company (the mentor) teams with your small SDVOSB (the protégé).
You can bid on contracts together as a joint venture. Your company gets real
past performance and experience. The mentor gets credit toward their small
business subcontracting goals. No money changes hands to participate.

This is the fastest path from zero federal contracts to a track record.

### Boots to Business — Military Spouses Can Attend
The free SBA entrepreneurship training course explicitly includes military
spouses and family members. Many of the businesses that come out of veteran
households are run by or built with spouses. They are eligible. Most don't know.

### SkillBridge Into Entrepreneurship
SkillBridge lets you work with a civilian organization for up to 180 days
before separation while still drawing military pay and benefits. Most people
use it to get a corporate job. Some use it to intern at a small business or
startup to learn operations before launching their own. Legal. Documented.
Almost nobody uses it this way.

### NAICS Code Stacking
Most veterans register at SAM.gov with one NAICS code. The right move is to
register with every code that applies to what your business can do.
More codes = more contract opportunities show up in searches.
A veteran-owned IT company that also does consulting, training, and logistics
should have 5–10 NAICS codes registered, not one.

### The 8(a) Program — Stackable with SDVOSB
The SBA's 8(a) Business Development program is for socially and economically
disadvantaged businesses. Veterans may qualify. 8(a) gives access to sole-source
contracts up to $4.5M (services) or $7M (manufacturing) — meaning the government
can award you a contract without competition at all.
A veteran who is also a minority and has an SC disability can hold SDVOSB + 8(a)
simultaneously. Check eligibility at sba.gov/8a.

### GSA Scale — Most Veterans Have No Idea
The federal government disposes of billions of dollars in assets annually.
Fleet vehicles, servers, medical equipment, construction tools, lab equipment,
aircraft parts, boats, furniture. Most of it is functional. Most of it goes
for fractions of market value. Veteran-owned businesses with active SAM.gov
registration and VOSB/SDVOSB certification get priority.
This is not a small program. It is enormous and almost entirely invisible to
most veterans.

## Tracks

### 1. SDVOSB / VOSB Certification

**Who qualifies:**
- Any veteran (honorable/general) who owns 51%+ and controls day-to-day ops → **VOSB**
- Same + service-connected disability (any rating) → **SDVOSB** (higher priority, Rule of Two applies)

**Where to certify:**
- SBA VetCert (vetcert.sba.gov) — unified portal for VOSB and SDVOSB; replaced old VA CVE process

**What you need:**
- DD-214
- Business formation documents
- Proof of 51%+ ownership
- If SDVOSB: VA SC disability rating letter

**Colorado state level:**
- CDVBE (Colorado Disabled Veteran Business Enterprise) — separate certification for CO state contracts
- Apply at: colorado.gov/pacific/dpa/vendor-certification

**Why this matters:** Without certification you cannot access set-aside contracts even if you
are the most qualified bidder. Certification is the key that opens the door.

---

### 2. Federal Contracting

**Required sequence:**
1. Register at SAM.gov — required before any federal bid; renew annually; free
2. Get NAICS codes — register all applicable codes, not just one
3. Build a Capability Statement — 1 page: what you do, certifications, NAICS codes, past performance, contact info
4. Research on USASpending.gov — who holds current contracts in your space, what agencies buy it

**Where to find set-aside opportunities:**
- SAM.gov — official federal contract opportunities, filter by SDVOSB/VOSB set-aside
- GSA eBuy (ebuy.gsa.gov) — quote requests for certified VOSBs
- VA contracting opportunities — specifically governed by Rule of Two

**Free help:**
- Colorado PTAP (coloradoptap.com) — specialized procurement technical assistance;
  free; call these people before doing anything else in contracting

**Advanced paths:**
- SBA Mentor-Protégé Program — solve the past performance problem (see above)
- HUBZone stacking — check map.sba.gov/hubzone/maps for your business address
- 8(a) application if additionally eligible

### 4. GSA Surplus & Auctions

**What comes through:** Fleet vehicles, servers, office equipment, construction tools,
scientific equipment, medical equipment, boats, aircraft parts, furniture.
Most is functional. Most goes well below market value.

**Where:**
- GSA Auctions (gsaauctions.gov) — register free; set category alerts for your industry
- GSA Xcess (gsaxcess.gov) — excess property before public auction; nonprofits and
  government entities get first access

**How to get priority:**
- Active SAM.gov registration
- VOSB/SDVOSB certification noted on profile
- DD-214 on file

**What to do right now:** Register at gsaauctions.gov, set up alerts for 3–5 categories
relevant to your business or personal use. Takes 10 minutes. Most veterans never do it.

## The Three Steps Every Eligible Veteran Should Take Immediately

1. **Get certified at vetcert.sba.gov** — VOSB at minimum; SDVOSB if SC disabled
2. **Register at SAM.gov** — required for any federal contract or GSA priority access; add all NAICS codes
3. **Register at gsaauctions.gov** — set up category alerts

None of these cost money. All three unlock programs most veterans never access.
